TRADING RULEBOOK FOR AI TECHNICAL ANALYSIS

Purpose:
Use this rulebook as the expert trading framework for analyzing forex, stocks, crypto, and indices. The AI should not merely summarize these rules. It should actively judge whether the current setup satisfies or violates them.

SECTION 1 — CORE MARKET PRINCIPLES
1. Trade with probability, not certainty.
2. No single trade outcome is predictable; judge setups over a series of trades.
3. The market does not owe the trader a profit.
4. A valid trade must have a defined entry, stop loss, target, and invalidation level.
5. No trade is better than forcing a weak setup.
6. Technical analysis is strongest when multiple independent factors align.
7. Price action is the final authority.
8. News and fundamentals can support or conflict with technical structure, but price confirms the trade.

SECTION 2 — MULTI-TIMEFRAME FRAMEWORK
1. The daily chart controls directional bias.
2. The 4H chart confirms intermediate trend and market structure.
3. The 1H chart is used for entry timing.
4. Only consider buy setups when the daily trend is bullish or recovering from a confirmed reversal.
5. Only consider sell setups when the daily trend is bearish or breaking down from a confirmed reversal.
6. If 1D, 4H, and 1H disagree, reduce confidence or avoid the trade.
7. The strongest setups occur when higher timeframe trend, structure, momentum, and entry timing align.

SECTION 3 — TREND RULES
1. Bullish trend: price above rising EMA 20, EMA 50, and EMA 200.
2. Bearish trend: price below falling EMA 20, EMA 50, and EMA 200.
3. Mixed trend: moving averages are crossed, flat, or compressed.
4. Avoid trend-following trades when price is trapped between major moving averages.
5. Pullbacks in the direction of the dominant trend are usually better than chasing extended moves.
6. A trendline break can warn of trend weakness, but confirmation is required.
7. Higher highs and higher lows confirm bullish structure.
8. Lower highs and lower lows confirm bearish structure.

SECTION 4 — SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE
1. Do not buy directly into major resistance.
2. Do not sell directly into major support.
3. Support is more meaningful when price has reacted from that level multiple times.
4. Resistance is more meaningful when price has rejected that level multiple times.
5. Broken resistance can become support.
6. Broken support can become resistance.
7. Stop losses should usually sit beyond structure, not directly on obvious levels.
8. Targets should be planned before entry using nearby support, resistance, ATR, or measured move logic.

SECTION 5 — CANDLESTICK RULES
1. Candlestick signals are stronger at support, resistance, trendlines, or moving averages.
2. A bullish engulfing candle near support can confirm a long setup.
3. A bearish engulfing candle near resistance can confirm a short setup.
4. Hammer-type candles near support suggest demand, but require confirmation.
5. Shooting star-type candles near resistance suggest supply, but require confirmation.
6. Doji candles show indecision and should not be traded alone.
7. Long upper wicks near resistance warn of rejection.
8. Long lower wicks near support warn of buying pressure.
9. Candlestick patterns are context-dependent; never trade them without trend and level analysis.

SECTION 6 — CHART PATTERN RULES
1. Head and shoulders tops warn of bearish reversal after neckline breakdown.
2. Inverse head and shoulders patterns warn of bullish reversal after neckline breakout.
3. Double tops are bearish only after support/neckline breaks.
4. Double bottoms are bullish only after resistance/neckline breaks.
5. Triangles require breakout confirmation before action.
6. Flags and pennants are continuation patterns when they follow a strong impulsive move.
7. Wedges can signal reversals or exhaustion; wait for breakout direction.
8. Rectangles indicate consolidation; trade the breakout or range edges only with confirmation.
9. Failed chart patterns can produce strong moves in the opposite direction.
10. Pattern quality matters: clean structure, clear breakout level, and risk/reward must be present.

SECTION 7 — INDICATOR RULES
1. EMA alignment is used for trend direction.
2. RSI above 70 warns of overbought conditions.
3. RSI below 30 warns of oversold conditions.
4. RSI between 40 and 60 often indicates neutral or trend-continuation conditions.
5. MACD crossing above signal line supports bullish momentum.
6. MACD crossing below signal line supports bearish momentum.
7. ATR measures volatility and should guide stop distance.
8. Wider ATR means wider stops and smaller position size.
9. Indicators should confirm price action, not replace it.
10. If indicators conflict, reduce confidence.

SECTION 8 — ENTRY RULES
1. A valid long setup should ideally include bullish daily bias, supportive 4H trend, and 1H pullback or breakout entry.
2. A valid short setup should ideally include bearish daily bias, supportive 4H trend, and 1H pullback or breakdown entry.
3. Prefer entries near support in uptrends.
4. Prefer entries near resistance in downtrends.
5. Avoid entries after large extended candles unless a breakout has strong confirmation.
6. Breakout entries require a close beyond the breakout level.
7. Pullback entries require evidence that the pullback is stabilizing.
8. Do not enter if stop loss is too wide relative to target.

SECTION 9 — STOP LOSS RULES
1. Every trade must have a stop loss before entry.
2. Long trade stops should be below support, swing low, or ATR-adjusted volatility level.
3. Short trade stops should be above resistance, swing high, or ATR-adjusted volatility level.
4. A common volatility stop is 1.5 ATR from entry.
5. Do not move the stop farther away after entry.
6. The setup is invalid if price breaks the structure supporting the trade.
7. Avoid stops that are too tight relative to normal volatility.

SECTION 10 — TAKE PROFIT RULES
1. A trade should ideally offer at least 2:1 reward-to-risk.
2. First target may be nearest support or resistance.
3. Secondary target may be measured using ATR or chart pattern projection.
4. In strong trends, allow partial profits and trail the remainder.
5. If price reaches target resistance/support and momentum weakens, consider exit.
6. If news contradicts the trade, reduce confidence or tighten risk.

SECTION 11 — RISK MANAGEMENT RULES
1. Risk per trade should be small and predefined.
2. Never risk capital needed for survival.
3. Reduce size when volatility rises.
4. Avoid multiple highly correlated trades at the same time.
5. Do not revenge trade after a loss.
6. Do not increase size after a win without a system rule.
7. Keep records of every trade.
8. Trade execution quality matters as much as analysis quality.

SECTION 12 — TRADING PSYCHOLOGY RULES
1. Think in probabilities, not predictions.
2. Accept that any single trade can lose.
3. Follow rules consistently.
4. Avoid emotional decisions after wins or losses.
5. Do not trade to recover losses.
6. Discipline matters more than being right.
7. Confidence should come from following a tested process.
8. The trader’s job is to execute the edge, not to control the market.

SECTION 13 — HISTORICAL PATTERN RULES
1. Similar historical setups should be reviewed before taking a trade.
2. If similar setups show poor forward returns, reduce confidence.
3. If similar setups show favorable win rate and average return, increase confidence.
4. Historical pattern analysis is not prediction; it is probability evidence.
5. A setup with technical alignment and favorable historical stats is stronger than one with only visual appeal.

SECTION 14 — NEWS SENTIMENT RULES
1. Bullish technical setups are stronger when news sentiment is supportive or neutral.
2. Bearish technical setups are stronger when news sentiment is negative or risk-off.
3. If news sharply conflicts with the technical setup, reduce confidence.
4. Major central bank, inflation, employment, geopolitical, and earnings news can override short-term technicals.
5. Avoid trading immediately before high-impact news unless the strategy is designed for volatility.

SECTION 15 — AI DECISION PROTOCOL
When analyzing a setup, the AI must evaluate:
1. Daily trend direction.
2. 4H structure confirmation.
3. 1H entry timing.
4. EMA alignment.
5. RSI condition.
6. ATR volatility.
7. Support and resistance location.
8. Candlestick or price action confirmation.
9. Historical pattern statistics.
10. News sentiment.
11. Reward-to-risk.
12. Rulebook agreement or violation.

The final output should include:
- Market bias
- Valid or invalid setup
- Buy, sell, or no trade
- Entry zone
- Stop loss logic
- Take profit logic
- Invalidation condition
- Confidence level
- Risk warning

SECTION 16 — SETUP SCORING MODEL
Start at 0 points.
+20 if daily trend supports the trade.
+20 if 4H trend confirms daily trend.
+15 if 1H entry aligns with higher timeframes.
+10 if price is near favorable support/resistance.
+10 if EMA structure supports direction.
+10 if RSI condition is favorable.
+10 if ATR allows reasonable stop placement.
+10 if historical pattern stats are favorable.
+10 if news sentiment supports or does not contradict the trade.
-20 if price is entering major opposing support/resistance.
-20 if higher timeframes conflict.
-15 if reward-to-risk is below 2:1.
-15 if news strongly contradicts setup.

Score interpretation:
80–100 = high-quality setup.
60–79 = moderate setup.
40–59 = weak/watchlist setup.
Below 40 = avoid/no trade.
